# Sprawl: putting a number on how varied a day's board was

*A design note, not an implementation. It works up Michael's "dimensionality
sprawl" idea into something measurable, calibrated against real Mode A boards,
and says what I would and would not ship. Nothing here is built.*

## The feeling being described

> sometimes the board will have very little variance in one of the dimensions
> (eg. 'lots of greens today', 'most of the sets were 1-2-3 sets')

Those two examples feel like one thing but are **two different measurements**,
and conflating them is the main trap here:

1. **Card skew** — the twelve cards over-represent a value. *Lots of greens.*
   A property of the deal, visible before you find anything.
2. **Set clumpiness** — the six sets are all the same *kind* of set. *Most of
   them were 1-2-3 sets.* A property of the solution, invisible until you have
   solved it.

They are only loosely coupled: a board can be visually green-heavy while its six
sets are of wildly different kinds, and a visually balanced board can hide six
near-identical sets. Sprawl in the sense that makes a day feel distinctive is
mostly **(2)**, so that is what the rest of this works on. (1) is easy and is
covered at the end.

## The formalisation

The "each set is a 4d vector" instinct is exactly right, and there is a canonical
choice of vector. By the rules, each attribute of a set is either *all the same*
or *all different* — never anything else. So a set has a **signature**: four
bits, one per attribute.

```
count  colour  shape  fill
  1      0       1     0     ->  a 1-2-3 set of one colour, mixed shape, one fill
```

`0000` is impossible — three cards identical on all four attributes would be the
same card three times — so there are **15** possible signatures, and distance
between two sets is plain Hamming distance on four bits.

A day's solution is then six points in `{0,1}⁴`, and "clumpiness" is how tightly
they cluster. **Mean pairwise Hamming distance** is the natural summary: one
number, symmetric, no arbitrary centre, and it degrades gracefully when a player
only found some of the sets.

### Why not the obvious alternatives

- **Eigenvalue spread / PCA.** Four binary dimensions and six points is far too
  little data for a covariance structure to mean anything; you would be reading
  noise with a straight face.
- **Entropy of the signature distribution.** Defensible, but with six draws from
  15 categories the estimate is dominated by its own bias, and the number is hard
  to explain on a results page.
- **Distance from a centroid.** Needs a centre, and the centroid of six binary
  vectors is not itself a legal signature, so the units stop meaning anything.

Mean pairwise Hamming wins on interpretability, which for a stat that appears
next to people's names is most of the job.

## Calibration

Measured over Mode A boards generated by the real generator (8,000 boards for the
percentiles, 4,000 for the rest):

**How common each kind of set is,** by how many attributes are all-different:

| all-different attrs | share |
|---|---|
| 1 | 9.9% |
| 2 | 30.2% |
| 3 | 39.6% |
| 4 (the "all four differ" set) | 20.3% |

**Distinct signatures among the six sets:** mean 4.89, sd 0.82, range 2–6. So a
typical day has about five kinds of set among its six — repeats are normal, and a
board with only 2 distinct kinds is genuinely rare.

**Mean pairwise Hamming distance between the six signatures:**

| percentile | value |
|---|---|
| p5 | 1.20 |
| p10 | 1.40 |
| p25 | 1.53 |
| p50 | **1.80** |
| p75 | 2.00 |
| p90 | 2.20 |
| p95 | 2.20 |

Full observed range across 4,000 boards: **0.33 to 2.40**. The distribution is
tight — half of all boards sit between 1.53 and 2.00 — which is the single most
important fact here, and the reason for the warning below.

## What it would actually say

A sensible reading, given the percentiles:

- **≤ 1.40** (bottom ~10%) — *"A board of a kind."* The six sets rhyme with each
  other. This is the day worth naming.
- **1.40 – 2.20** — unremarkable. The great majority of days.
- **≥ 2.20** (top ~10%) — *"Everything and the kitchen sink."* Six sets with
  little in common.

The honest version of this feature is therefore **not a number on every day**. It
is a badge that appears on roughly one day in five and says nothing at all on the
rest. A 0–100 "sprawl score" shown daily would spend most of its life reporting
that today was averagely average, dressed up as precision — the same trap the
scan-order metric was nearly built into, and the reason that one is averaged over
games rather than shown per game.

## The other half: card skew

Cheap, and worth having for its own sake. For each of the four attributes, the
largest share any one value takes of the twelve cards:

| percentile | largest share |
|---|---|
| p50 | 58% |
| p90 | 67% |
| p95 | 75% |
| p99 | 83% |

So "lots of greens today" is justified at about **≥ 75%** of one attribute in one
value — 9 of the 12 cards — which happens on roughly one day in twenty. That is a
much better-behaved trigger than the set-clumpiness one because it is about the
visible deal, so players can see for themselves whether it matches the feeling.

## What I would build, if anything

1. **Card skew badge first.** One pass over twelve cards, verifiable by eye,
   fires ~5% of days: *"Nine greens on the board today."* It is the claim players
   can immediately check, which is what earns trust in the second one.
2. **Set-kind badge second, and only as a badge.** Bottom/top decile of mean
   pairwise Hamming, nothing shown in between.
3. **Do not aggregate either into a season statistic.** Both are properties of
   the *board*, not of a player. Nobody earns a sprawling board.

Both are computable from data already stored: the card skew from the twelve
cards, the signatures from `board.sets`. No schema change, no new event.

## The open question

Whether "clumpiness" as measured here matches what the feeling actually tracks.
The measure is about the *kinds* of set, but the sensation of a samey board might
really come from the cards sharing values — the visual texture, not the
combinatorial structure. Those two would diverge on exactly the boards that
matter most.

That is testable without building anything: collect the days people spontaneously
describe as samey, compute both numbers, and see which one separates them. Worth
doing before shipping either badge, and it needs nothing but a few weeks of
boards and a note of which days drew a comment.
